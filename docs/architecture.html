How the Funda Score is calculated

Pure arithmetic in core/scoring.py, using sector rules from core/sectors.py. The same file and sector always give the same score.

Take 12 ratiosFor each, use the latest year and the average of the last 3 years.
Compare with the sector's linesEach sector has its own "weak" and "strong" line per ratio. Debt of 2× is fine for a bank, alarming for software.
Turn into 1–100At the weak line = 40. At the strong line = 66. In between, a straight line. Beyond them it keeps going to 1 or 100.
Blend and nudge60% latest year + 40% three-year average, plus a small nudge for whether the ratio is improving or slipping.
Roll upRatios → 5 pillars (average) → one score, weighted by what matters in that sector.
WEAK
NEUTRAL
STRONG
40 = sector's weak line
66 = sector's strong line

The 12 ratios and their pillars

PillarRatios
GrowthSales growth · Net profit growth
ProfitabilityEBITDA margin · Net profit margin
ReturnsROE · ROCE · ROA
LeverageDebt to equity (lower is better) · Interest coverage
EfficiencyCash conversion cycle (lower is better) · CFO / PAT · Fixed asset turnover

The formula

ratio score = 0.6 × score(latest year) + 0.4 × score(3-year average) + trend nudge (kept within 1–100) pillar score = average of its ratio scores total = Σ (pillar score × sector weight) ÷ Σ sector weights cash check : 3-yr CFO/PAT below 0.5 → −5 3-yr CFO/PAT above 1.0 → +2.5 verdict : 66 or more → STRONG below 40 → WEAK otherwise → NEUTRAL
A ratio that is missing from the workbook is first derived from the statements (core/derive.py). If it still cannot be worked out it is listed as a data gap and left out — it is never guessed or set to zero. There are 9 sector rule sets; the app picks one automatically from the company name and its numbers, and you can change it.

Sector Lens maths

The sector figure is not an average of ratios. Totals are added up across all peers first, then the ratio is taken, so a large company carries its real weight.

From the Screener snapshot

Bottom-up sector totals (core/screener.py)

Equity = Equity capital + Reserves EBIT = Operating profit − Depreciation Capital employed = Equity + Borrowings Sector P/E = Σ market cap ÷ Σ net profit (loss-makers left out) Sector P/B = Σ market cap ÷ Σ equity Sector ROE = Σ net profit ÷ Σ equity Sector ROCE = Σ EBIT ÷ Σ capital employed

Banks and finance companies: ROCE is shown as "—" because it does not apply.

From the monthly IndianAPI snapshot

Pooled fundamentals and the cycle tilt (core/indianapi.py, core/tilt.py)

  • Sector ROA = Σ net income ÷ Σ total assets
  • PEG, EPS growth, Piotroski score, top 10 by market cap (no extra API calls)
  • Month-on-month change in earnings growth, revenue growth, ROE, ROA and valuation
  • From those changes, one tilt word: Expansion, Recovery, Stable, Mid-cycle, Slowdown or Mixed

Your company is then placed against these: the gap bars, the positioning chart and the "priced above / earning below" headline.

If a peer is missing from the day's snapshot, the app fetches just that one company live from Screener and keeps it for 6 hours. Anything a source does not provide stays blank and is shown as "—".

The AI layer

The numbers are decided before the AI is called. It receives the finished figures and writes about them in plain words.

What it writes
  • The analyst note on the Dashboard (1 call)
  • The model interpretation, in 3 parts at once (3 calls)
  • The sector's current-cycle read (1 call)
  • Company news summaries (1 call)
  • Each question you ask (1 call each)
How it is called
  • Groq is tried first (default model openai/gpt-oss-120b)
  • Gemini takes over if Groq fails or is rate-limited (gemini-flash-latest)
  • Several keys are rotated so parallel jobs use different keys
  • Failed calls are retried a few times, then give up cleanly
The guard rails
  • AI does no arithmetic and cannot change a ratio or the score
  • It may only summarise real, retrieved news — never invent an event
  • No AI key? Every page still works, using text written from the scoring rules
  • One writing rule book for all text: plain words, what the number means

Automations

Two robots run on GitHub's servers and save their results back into the project as files. The app picks the new files up on its next update.

Robot 1 · live Screener snapshot

About every 5 minutes while the market is open

  • When: Monday–Friday, 09:15 to 15:30 IST (GitHub may delay or skip some runs)
  • Does: reads each of the 410 peer companies once from Screener, works out the sector totals
  • Writes: data/screener_snapshot.json and data/screener_history/<date>.json
  • If it fails: the previous snapshot is kept; a company that failed reuses its last value, marked stale

File: .github/workflows/daily-screener-refresh.yml → scripts/refresh_screener.py

Robot 2 · monthly sector snapshot

1st of every month, 08:30 IST

  • Does first: downloads the latest official NSE index member lists
  • Then: fetches fundamentals from IndianAPI and computes pooled metrics, top 10, month-on-month change and tilt
  • Credit guard: counts the requests first and aborts before any call if it would exceed 400; stops at once on a login or rate-limit error
  • Writes: data/sector_snapshot.json, only after it passes validation

File: .github/workflows/sector-snapshot.yml → scripts/fetch_nse_constituents.py, scripts/refresh_sectors.py

What happens when you upload a file

In order. Steps 1–4 take about a second; the AI and news work in step 5 runs in the background while you already see the pages.

STEP 1
ReadFind the sheets, match line names through the synonym list, build clean yearly tables.
STEP 2
Fill gapsAny ratio the workbook lacks is derived from the income statement and balance sheet.
STEP 3
Pick sectorDetected from the company name and its numbers (you can change it). Sets the weak/strong lines and weights.
STEP 4
Score12 ratios → 5 pillars → Funda Score and verdict. Live price overlaid on the header.
STEP 5
Background jobs4 AI write-ups start at once; then the Sector Lens data (prices, cycle read, news) is prepared.
STEP 6
Show pagesPages that need no AI open immediately. A page that needs a job waits only for that job.
